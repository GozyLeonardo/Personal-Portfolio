An engineering firm that builds precision machines had a website that looked like a filing cabinet.

The work was serious — prequalification, compliance, capability statements. But the site presented it the way a document presents it, flat and grey, as if the firm had never built anything worth showing. The gap between the company and the front was the whole problem. People trust an engineering firm with infrastructure, and the site did not look trustworthy.

So I rebuilt it in two days.

## What I built

A futuristic high-tech theme. An SVG logo drawn from scratch. A preloader so the site arrives with intent, not a flash of unstyled content. A landing page that feels like the machine shop it represents — precise, engineered, deliberate.

The foundation underneath was a Proof Kit — the documentation layer that actually matters for an engineering firm: project documentation, a capability statement, engineer profiles, a compliance tracker. The website is the front. The Proof Kit is what wins the contract. I built both.

## What broke

Two days is not a lot of time for a full redesign, and the pressure showed in the corners. The preloader needed tuning to land on every page without feeling repetitive. The logo needed to read at small sizes as clearly as it did large. Each of those is a small thing that eats an hour.

## What I learned

The deadline was the discipline. Two days forces you to decide what the site is actually for — and for an engineering firm, it is not to be beautiful. It is to look like it can be trusted with the work. A flat site does not communicate that. A precise one does.

The front and the proof are the same thing. The site has to look like the capability statement before anyone reads it.
