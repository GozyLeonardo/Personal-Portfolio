A family's history should not live on a platform that can delete it.

That was the brief, underneath the brief. The request was a website. The need was something sturdier — a digital home where a legacy could sit for decades, owned, not rented. So I built Benlaz as a place the family controls, not a page they visit.

## What I built

A Next.js site backed by Sanity — a real CMS, not a static page. Three content types that map to how a family actually thinks: the people (family members), the moments (a gallery), and the words (a journal). Each one editable without touching code.

On top of that, NextAuth with a proper admin dashboard. The family members who are not engineers can still log in, see the contacts, and manage what goes public. The site is not a monument. It is a living thing.

Then I hardened it. Timing-safe auth so a login can't be timed. Edge middleware to guard the routes. CSP headers. CSRF protection. An XSS fix in the email notifications. I patched eight security holes in one pass, the kind of thing you do not notice until the day you are glad you did.

## What broke

The homepage fought me the entire way. It wanted to be a hero-only page; every navigation change pulled the family grid back in, then hid it again. I reverted the copy once, restored the nav twice, and ended up splitting the whole thing: a hero-only front door, the full navigation behind it. The logo kept flipping between "Benlaz Family" and something generic, and I had to nail it down three times.

Framer Motion's type system did not like my animation variants. The ease tuples needed an explicit cast before TypeScript would stop complaining. Minor, but it stalled every build until I got it right.

## What I learned

A site for a family is not a portfolio piece. It is a responsibility. The security work was not optional polish — it was the point. When the thing you are building has to outlast you, you build it like a vault, not like a flyer.

And a CMS is only as useful as the people who can actually use it. If the family needs a developer to write a memory, the CMS failed.
