An events company sells a feeling before it sells a date.

But their site read like a spec sheet. The copy was fine, technically. It just did not feel like the thing it was selling — no warmth, no occasion, no hint that a wedding or a launch party was waiting on the other side. You could read the whole thing and never once imagine the room with the lights on it.

So I rebuilt it around the feeling.

## What I built

First, the copy. I rewrote it in plain language — not corporate, not ornate, just words a person would actually say when planning the biggest day of their life. The language had to sound like a conversation, not a contract.

Then the ornament. I built a floral system from scratch — eight botanical variants, each a different composition, spread across the site so no two sections repeat the same arrangement. It was not decoration slapped on top. It was the thing that carried the occasion, the way a flower arrangement carries a table.

Underneath it all, the tooling: a job-costing calculator moved into a private admin hub, and a Decap CMS so the client could edit their own content without calling me.

## What broke

The florals were too timid on the first pass. They existed, but they did not carry — the compositions repeated, the feeling was muted. I had to make them bolder and push the variants across the whole site, not just the home page. Restraint is right until it becomes invisible. Then it is not restraint, it is absence.

## What I learned

The copy and the ornament are doing the same job — they are setting the mood before the reader knows they are being sold. Plain language is not dumbing down. It is the highest form of polish: words chosen so precisely they do not need ornament to land.

An events site is not a brochure. It is a first impression of the event itself.
