A product front should not look like a template wearing your name.

Four products. One signal. And a design language that had to feel like it came from somewhere — not from a Figma library, not from a SaaS template, but from the ground the whole thing was built on. I called it Cosmic African Soul, and I built the Signal's front on it in four days.

## What I built

Four landing pages, one per direction, all on the same design system. The system was the real work: Igbo-Ukwu interlacing circles as the primary geometry, Uli curves as dividers, Nsibidi glyphs as section markers, Foundation Black as the field everything sits on.

Then I amplified it until you could not miss it. The first pass was too timid — the geometry was there but you had to squint. The second pass made it load-bearing: visible circles, a star field, motifs you feel before you read.

On top of the front, I added the Signal Tools — five everyday problem-solving tools. Not the products themselves. The entry point. The thing that gets someone to stay long enough to see what the products are.

## What broke

The brand color kept trying to drift. I caught a gradient sneaking in — a midpoint color that was not the flat gold the brand demanded. Then a second one, a slightly wrong hex. Each time the fix was the same: strip the gradient, lock it to pure `#C47A00`. A brand color is not a suggestion. It is a law, and gradients are how you break the law without noticing.

## What I learned

A design system is not a palette. It is a boundary. The moment you let one gradient through, you have let the template back in.

The four days were not about building four pages. They were about building one thing — a face — that would hold up across everything else the Signal becomes. Get the face right once, and every product that follows inherits it for free.
