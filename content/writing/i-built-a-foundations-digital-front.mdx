A foundation that exists to help people should not be split across two competing doors.

It had two domains — `.com` and `.org` — and no single face. Two URLs, two versions of the same story, both claiming to be the real one. Search engines split the authority. Donors saw one name one day and another the next. A foundation is an institution. Institutions have one front door.

So I built it one.

## What I built

The landing page and the technical architecture first — the institutional foundation a real organization needs, not a template. Research, structure, then the build. A preloader that carries the motto the right way.

Then the security pass. A care foundation handles people's stories, and those stories deserve to be protected — input validation, hardening, the works.

The domain was the quiet victory. I pointed `.org` and `www` at the canonical `.com`, so every path leads to one door. One name. One front.

## What broke

The motto kept leaking out too early. The preloader was supposed to reveal "Hope for the Hopeless" at the right moment, but on first load it flashed the line before the animation was ready. The fix was not to remove the line — it was to hold it until the moment earned it. A motto is a reveal, not a header.

## What I learned

One front door is not a technical decision. It is an institutional one. The domain that wins is the identity that wins, and a foundation's identity is the thing it cannot afford to have divided.

The security work was not a checklist. It was the same principle as the domain — the people who come to a care foundation are trusting it with their story, and that trust is the entire asset.
