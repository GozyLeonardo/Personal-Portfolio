My site was cosplay.

Not the kind I accuse other people of — the polite kind. It talked about a $500T empire and the soul of Africa. It said "every line of code is a small act of return." It stacked aphorisms the way a cathedral stacks stone.

It showed nothing that proved the work was real.

That is the brutal part. I had been writing "most African tech is cosplay — Western software wearing African colours, built by people who have never had to use it." Then I looked at my own front door and realized it was doing the same thing. Cosmic language. No evidence. A talker, not a doer.

Today I rebuilt it.

## The rewrite

I rewrote every page in present-continuous tense. Not "I build" — "I am building." Not "the work will be" — "the work is running." The past shows up in exactly one place: as a lesson, and only when it leads to now.

The blunt thesis moved into the headline. "Most African tech is cosplay. I am building the real thing." No hedge. No sympathy. Name it, then show it.

The vagueness went first. "Ala holds the dead" reads beautiful and says nothing. I cut it. What stayed is the line that carries weight: eleven live builds. Six in progress. Here is the URL, go look.

## The inventory

The copy only works if the numbers are true. So I stopped writing and started scanning — every folder and every file across client work, family lab, and the personal sandbox. Not the folder names. The files inside them.

What I found was not what the site claimed. The site said six ventures. The filesystem said eleven live deployments and six in build, plus a stack of own-product work in various states of progress. Some finished. Some empty folders with a name and nothing else.

Worse — six of the links I had put on the site pointed at stale hashed `.vercel.app` URLs. Per-deployment addresses that die the moment the next build goes out. The real production URLs were sitting one command away, returning 200, and I had linked to the wrong thing.

I fixed all six. I verified all thirteen resolve. Athena — the AI orchestration layer running behind the builds — got added as a live build. Chi stayed parked, because parked is the truth.

## The infrastructure

Then the infrastructure broke my flow, which is what infrastructure does.

The GitHub repo had been renamed. `lawrencenwuzor.com` became `Personal-Portfolio`. GitHub still redirects the old URL, so the pushes kept working — but Vercel's git integration went silent. Every push was landing, and nothing was auto-deploying. I had been deploying by hand and not noticing the gap.

I relinked the remote, reconnected Vercel to the renamed repo, and pushed a real commit to prove it. The deployment fired on its own. That is the difference between "the system works" and "I know the system works."

While I was in there, I found a stale duplicate project on Vercel — the Sparknexis site from before it had a domain, still occupying a slot and a URL. I verified the live domain was attached to the correct project, not the duplicate, and deleted it. Clean.

## The polish

The design is dark first. Foundation Black is the material of the universe — that is not decoration, it is the architecture. But a site needs a daylight register, so I added a light/dark toggle. Same soul on paper: Warm Off-White becomes the field, Foundation Black becomes the ink, the gold and teal darken for contrast.

The one thing that had to survive the switch was the Okavango pattern — the triangle tessellation that runs behind everything. The first light-mode pass dropped it. That is a bug, not a choice. I restored it with the darkened gold so it stays visible on the light field.

Then SEO. Every page had a title and a description and a canonical. Nine pages were leaning on the site-wide OpenGraph fallback — the same image and the same title on every social share. I added per-page OpenGraph to all nine. A shared post about the build log should not say "About this site."

## What I learned

The diagnosis I level at African tech applies to my own front door first. Cosmic language without evidence is just cosplay with better fonts. I was writing about a civilizational build while the proof sat in folders I had not opened in weeks.

Proof is the only voice that carries. Not the vision. Not the aphorism. The URL that returns 200.

Eleven live builds. Six in progress. One thesis. And a site that finally shows the work instead of describing it.
