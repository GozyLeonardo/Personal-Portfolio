A corporate site where the styles do not load is worse than no site at all.

That was the state of it. The CSS pipeline was broken, so the page rendered half-dressed — content there, styling gone, the whole thing looking like it had been abandoned mid-load. A stuck preloader sat on top of it, trapping visitors on a loading screen that never ended. For an ICT firm, a company that sells technology competence, this was fatal. It told every visitor the firm could not even keep its own front working.

So I rebuilt it from the stylesheet up.

## What I built

First, the broken pipeline. I ripped it out and rebuilt it clean — real brand assets instead of placeholders, the display font swapped, the business branding brightened so it did not read like a government form.

Then the contact form. The old one did not actually work — it was a decoration. I wired it to send, so a lead stopped being a hope and became a message in an inbox.

The preloader was the last stubborn piece. It kept sticking, holding the page hostage. I made it CSS-driven so it starts on first paint — the moment the page begins, the loader begins, and it hands off cleanly instead of blocking the content forever.

## What broke

The font swap changed the whole personality of the page, and it took a minute to get right — a display font carries more weight than people think. And the preloader fix meant rewriting the entrance so it was not tied to a JavaScript event that could fire late or never. The lesson was the same in both: the page has to start on its own, without waiting for a script to give it permission.

## What I learned

A broken site is not a design problem. It is a credibility problem. For a firm selling competence, the front is the first and loudest piece of evidence — and a stuck loading screen says "incompetent" before a single word of copy gets read.

Fix the pipeline before you touch the pixels. Everything else is decoration on a floor that has to hold first.
